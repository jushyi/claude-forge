Usage: forge concern add <repo> <area> <severity> <files...> -- <text>

Record a new open concern in <repo>'s .forge/concerns.json, with the given
area, files, severity (high, medium or low) and text, no evidence, and
today's date as YYYY-MM-DD. Its id is the next C-NN: the highest existing
id number plus one, at least two digits, so C-01 in an empty or missing
concerns.json and C-100 after C-99. A missing concerns.json is created.
Re-renders CONCERNS.md, which lists open concerns only, and commits both
files as "docs(concerns): add <repo>:C-NN" under the repo's <git common
dir>/forge.lock, leaving any other staged change uncommitted. Prints
"added <repo>:C-NN".

<repo> resolves as forge resolve resolves it: a child directory of the
project root holding .git, else the project root under its basename. The
concern is always written to that repo's main checkout, never to the
worktree forge runs in, so a concern found in a phase worktree is not lost
with its branch. Record new concerns with this command, never by editing
CONCERNS.md. CONCERNS.md lists open concerns only; a resolved concern stays
in concerns.json and drops out of CONCERNS.md when it is re-rendered.

Exits 2 on bad usage: no add, no --, no file, an empty area or text, or a
severity outside high, medium and low. Fails, writing nothing, on an
unknown repo or a concerns.json that is not valid JSON.

Reads:
  <main checkout>/.forge/concerns.json

Writes:
  <main checkout>/.forge/concerns.json
  <main checkout>/.forge/CONCERNS.md

Example:
  forge concern add acme-hub intake high src/intake.ts src/form.ts -- 'Intake trusts the client email unchecked'
